03

Three routes

All three read as an infinity figure. They differ in how the figure is constructed — as a scanned field, as hard geometry, or as a single twisted surface. That construction logic is what the rest of the system inherits, so this is the real decision.

Route A
Field

The lemniscate resolved as horizontal scan lines, densest at the crossing. Reads as resolution, signal, a thing being measured rather than drawn.

+Most distinctive of the three. Nothing in the category looks like it.
+Generative: the line pitch becomes a whole system of patterns.
−Collapses to grey mush below ~64 px. Needs a separate solid cut.
−Embroidery and single-colour print are genuinely difficult.
Hold as alternate
Route B
Meridian

Two squares on a 45° grid, overlapped so they share one node. The shared node is the client relationship — the part both parties stand in.

+Survives anywhere: fax, embroidery, engraving, 16 px favicon.
+Rigid construction makes it trivially easy to redraw correctly.
−Rotated-square marks are crowded territory in enterprise software.
−Reads "partnership" more than it reads "infinite".
Hold as alternate
Route C
Continuum

One ribbon with a single half-twist. Follow the surface and you return to where you started having crossed to the other side — without ever leaving the band.

+On-name without being a keyboard ∞. The twist is the argument.
+Two tones carry the gradient system naturally.
+Holds from 16 px to a building wrap with one drawing.
−Closest to the expected answer. Execution has to earn it.
Recommended

The honest objection

An infinity mark for a company called Infinite is the first thing anyone would draw. We think that is the point. The lemniscate is unownable as an idea and very ownable as a drawing — the equity comes from this specific band, this specific twist, this specific pair of tones, used relentlessly.

The test we set it

Cover the wordmark. Put the mark on a slide beside the other three firms. If a buyer can still say which one is Infinite a week later, the mark works. Route C is the only one we would put through that test at 16 px and at 16 metres.

05

The mark

A single band, given one half-twist and closed on itself. It has one surface and one edge — trace it with a finger and you come back to the start having covered both faces without ever crossing a seam. That is a Möbius band, and it is the cleanest available picture of work that has no handover point.

06

What each decision is doing

Four choices, each one arguable. If a stakeholder wants to overturn one, this is the page to argue on.

The half-twist
Not decoration

A plain figure-eight is two loops that touch. A Möbius band is one loop that changes side. The difference is exactly the difference between a vendor and a partner: both keep going, only one of them is still the same surface at the far end.

One ramp, two states
Dimmed, not cut

A single left-to-right ramp — sky, cobalt, iris, orchid — runs across the whole mark, and both halves sample it at the same point, so the colour never breaks at the join. The receding half is that same ramp lifted toward white as the surface turns away. The only tonal step left is at the crossing itself, which is exactly where one surface passes in front of another.

The band narrows at the crossing
Optical, not mathematical

Width tapers to 42% at the centre. A constant-width band goes visually heavy where the two halves meet and the mark turns into a blob. The taper is what keeps the crossing readable at small sizes. Flattened to one colour, it still holds.

It is wider than it is tall
1.615 : 1

Close to the golden ratio, and close to the proportion of a business card, a slide and a browser tab. The mark was drawn to the containers it has to live in, not to a square that gets cropped later.

09

Misuse

Every one of these has happened to a real identity inside its first year. They are listed so the answer is already written down when someone asks.

✕Never rotate. The band reads as a surface only when it sits level.
✕Never stretch. Scale proportionally or not at all.
✕Never recolour outside the palette. Cool is the whole differentiator.
✕Never tint the front half. Front is always full strength.
✕Never add shadow, bevel or glow. The twist already carries the depth.
Infinite
✕Never crowd the mark. Clear space is a minimum, not a target.

The gradient — covers and icons only

One rule worth writing down

The mono face is never used for eyebrows, headings or pull quotes. It is for figures a reader might check — uptime, dates, coordinates, version numbers. The moment it becomes decorative, the system reads as a developer's portfolio rather than a firm that ships for banks.

Voice

The mark promises continuity. The copy has to sound like a firm that has been doing this for a while and does not need to shout — specific, unhurried, and willing to name a number.

14

Open items

Things this document has decided provisionally and should not be treated as settled.

Legal name

The lockup is set as Infinite over the tagline Design. Develop. Refine. — a tagline, not a legal descriptor. Confirm the registered trading name and whether it must still appear in full on contracts, invoices and the website footer.

Trademark

Lemniscate marks are densely registered. A clearance search in the relevant classes is required before any spend on signage or stationery. The specific band construction is defensible; the general idea is not.

Competitor read

Section 01 describes category patterns from observation. Verify against the current Devsinc, 10Pearls and TKXEL sites before this deck goes in front of anyone external.

No dark theme

This identity is committed to light ground — ink on paper, screen second. That is a decision, not an oversight. A dark system is a separate piece of work and should be scoped only if the product side needs it.

Typeface

Figtree is open-licensed and free to deploy everywhere. If the brand wants a face nobody else can have, budget for a licensed or drawn alternative — the geometry of the mark does not depend on it.

Motion

The band has an obvious animation: the twist travelling around the loop, once, on load. Not drawn here. Worth prototyping before the website brief.
